City Cost Accuracy Report

Across 38 experiments, the best method cut the typical error in Holiday Spend's new-city price estimates to about 18%, and the gain held on 142 cities no experiment had used. This report sets out what to ship, what did not work, what to be careful about, and the decisions that are yours.

Errors are the budget-weighted mean absolute log error per city on the R3 panel (333 development cities, actual booking-site hotel prices); ≈% is eerror − 1. Method details and every experiment are on the hill-climb page.

Recommendation

Ship bundle 022 for newly generated cities. It keeps the v1.1 contract (the model returns ten USD prices and an exchange-rate observation; the server does all arithmetic) and changes three things:

  1. Model: GPT-6 Luna at maximum reasoning, the current production default.
  2. Prompt: v1.1 plus up to five web searches for restaurant, coffee and drink prices (six searches in total with the exchange rate). Hotels and hostels remain the model's own estimates.
  3. Correction: a fixed table of multipliers per price item and country, leaning on the region when a country has few reference cities, applied to the USD prices on the server before the tier formulas. The table is learnt once from the reference panel and versioned with the method.

Limits to keep in mind

  • Food and drink accuracy is partly the model finding the reference source. Food and drink references come from Numbeo, and Luna's searches can read Numbeo. For cocktails this was measured directly: with search, a third of Luna's prices were within 2% of Expatistan's, against 8% without. The app still gets sensible crowd-sourced local prices, but the 7–10% food errors overstate accuracy against what travellers actually pay.
  • Hotels are the remaining problem, and it is a ranking problem. After correction the overall hotel price level is right; what is left is not knowing which cities are dearer than others. Neither searching, comparable cities nor correction supplied that. A real improvement would need hotel prices looked up per city at generation time.
  • The 3-star references have their own noise. Booking.com and Trip.com disagree by about 13% for a typical city, which puts the measurable floor for 3-star error near 0.11.
  • The hostel private-room reference is the weakest in the panel (Hostelworld's lowest "from" prices for near-term dates, doubled, grade C).
  • Only newly generated cities are covered. The 121-city CSV and older v1 cities keep their stored values.

These are large, visible changes. Correction sets each price item to the level its reference defines. For the 3-star room that matches your decision of 16 October (actual 3-star booking prices). For hostels, the reference is a near-minimum price, so applying its factor would put hostel budgets near the cheapest listing rather than a typical night. The v1 formulas also derive the 4-star tier as 3-star × 1.80, so correcting 3-star lowers 4-star by the same proportion.

Keeping prices current

  • Exchange rates: recompute AUD values from the stored USD prices at the latest RBA rate, with no model call.
  • Inflation: a yearly uplift by country from free World Bank or IMF price indexes, since each city's generation date.
  • Hotel price level: once a year, recollect booking prices for 100–150 reference cities and refit the correction table. This updates every city's hotel level at once; rerunning cities with the same model does not.
  • New model: re-measure on the panel and refit before adopting, as the project already requires.

Decisions for you

  1. Adopt bundle 022 for new cities? Recommended. It is confirmed on held-out cities and keeps the v1.1 contract, with v1.1 and v1 as rollbacks.
  2. Which correction factors to apply. Recommended: food and drink (small, safe), and 3-star (your 16 October decision). Hold 1-star and hostels until their price definitions are settled, or apply all eight for maximum measured accuracy.
  3. 4-star tier. Let it follow the corrected 3-star (× 1.80), or decouple it with its own definition.
  4. Existing cities. Leave them, or regenerate the 527-city library under the new method (one Luna call per city). Older v1 cities need a regeneration before they can follow exchange-rate updates.
  5. Maintenance. Build the exchange-rate recompute first (code only), then schedule the yearly reference refresh.
